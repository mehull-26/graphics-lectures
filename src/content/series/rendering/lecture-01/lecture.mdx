## What this lecture covers

- Why radiometry is the right language for light transport.
- The relationship between flux, irradiance, and radiance (see <EqRef id="eq-irradiance" /> and <EqRef id="eq-radiance" />).
- How these quantities appear in rendering equations.

Understanding the geometry of light transport is essential. As shown in <FigRef id="fig-radiometry-diagram" />, we measure light from different perspectives depending on what question we're asking.

<Details summary="Derivation" icon="🔬" variant="derivation">

Starting from flux $\Phi$, irradiance over a differential area is defined by <EqRef id="eq-irradiance" />.

<Equation id="eq-irradiance" number="1">

$$
E = \frac{d\Phi}{dA}
$$

</Equation>

For directional dependence, we introduce radiance (<EqRef id="eq-radiance" />):

<Equation id="eq-radiance" number="2">

$$
L = \frac{d^2\Phi}{dA \, d\omega \, \cos\theta}
$$

</Equation>

These two fundamental quantities form the basis of all light transport calculations.

</Details>
